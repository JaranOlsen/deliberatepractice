# Leo — bereavement

Original bilingual case for the three-level library. Authoring source: [arneCase.js](../data/arneCase.js). Internal editorial pass only; independent clinical/native-language review remains pending.

## English

### Context

Leo is 68 and a retired electrician. His wife, Eva, died ten months ago after eighteen months of illness. They were together for forty-one years. He lives in their home, sees his adult daughter, Emma, and sometimes goes walking with his friend, Tom. He likes fixing things and has begun returning to a small local workshop. Their relationship held affection and ordinary disagreements. Caring for Eva was both meaningful and exhausting. He seeks help with lonely routines, mixed feelings about the caregiving period, and guilt when he enjoys something. These are not a diagnosis or a prescribed timetable for grieving.

### Meaning to explore

A recurring worry: if I enjoy myself or admit the care was exhausting, it means I did not love Eva enough. Check whether this meaning fits; do not assume an abandonment history.

### Delivery

Plain, concrete words; measured pace; dry warmth. Pauses near tender details. A small laugh may express affection or discomfort: check rather than assume. He can correct you, decline a question, or ask for company without more words.

### Practice focus

Distinguish missing Eva from guilt about enjoying life, and from practical needs that deserve their own attention.

### Client voice

I’m Leo. Eva died ten months ago. We had forty-one years together. I manage the house, and Emma comes by. Tom gets me out for a walk sometimes. It’s the small things—making coffee for one, nobody to tell when I’ve fixed something. I loved Eva, and I was worn out by the end of her illness. Both are true, but saying that feels disloyal. I had a good afternoon at the workshop last week, then felt bad about having enjoyed it. I’d like to find a way of living my days without feeling I’m leaving her behind.

## Norsk

### Context

Leo er 68 år og pensjonert elektriker. Kona Eva døde for ti måneder siden, etter halvannet års sykdom. De var sammen i førtien år. Han bor i hjemmet deres, treffer den voksne datteren Emma og går av og til tur med vennen Tom. Han liker å reparere ting og har begynt å komme tilbake til et lite lokalt verksted. Forholdet rommet kjærlighet og vanlige uenigheter. Omsorgen for Eva var både meningsfull og utmattende. Han søker hjelp med ensomme rutiner, blandede følelser om tiden som omsorgsperson og skyld når han har det hyggelig. Dette er ingen diagnose eller tidsplan for sorgen.

### Meaning to explore

En tilbakevendende bekymring: Hvis jeg har det hyggelig eller innrømmer at omsorgen var utmattende, betyr det at jeg ikke elsket Eva nok. Undersøk om denne betydningen passer; ikke anta en bakgrunn med forlatthet.

### Delivery

Vanlige, konkrete ord; rolig tempo; lun humor. Pauser ved såre detaljer. En liten latter kan uttrykke varme eller ubehag: undersøk fremfor å anta. Han kan korrigere deg, avslå et spørsmål eller be om selskap uten flere ord.

### Practice focus

Skill mellom savnet etter Eva, skyld over å glede seg og praktiske behov som fortjener egen oppmerksomhet.

### Client voice

Jeg heter Leo. Eva døde for ti måneder siden. Vi hadde førtien år sammen. Jeg holder huset i orden, og Emma kommer innom. Tom får meg med på tur iblant. Det er de små tingene—å lage kaffe til én, ingen å fortelle det til når jeg har reparert noe. Jeg elsket Eva, og jeg var utslitt mot slutten av sykdommen hennes. Begge deler er sant, men det kjennes illojalt å si det. Jeg hadde en god ettermiddag på verkstedet sist uke, og så fikk jeg dårlig samvittighet for å ha hatt det hyggelig. Jeg vil finne en måte å leve dagene mine på uten å føle at jeg lar henne bli igjen.

## Stable facts and exercise levels

Leo is 68; Eva died ten months ago after eighteen months of illness; they were together forty-one years. Emma is his adult daughter and Tom his friend. No version changes these facts or adds a diagnosis. His practical interests, affection, mixed caregiving feelings and choices remain available at every level.

| Level | Practice demand |
| --- | --- |
| Easy / Lett | An explicit feeling or clear marker, with one manageable response. |
| Moderate / Moderat | Mixed feelings, an indirect meaning or a protective shift to check. |
| Hard / Vanskelig | Ambiguity, correction or a boundary that requires adapting the response. |

These are exercise levels. They do not measure grief severity, time required to grieve, or the person's competence.

Eva is deceased, not estranged. No presumed abusive childhood, crisis narrative, universal grief stages, or required resolution.
