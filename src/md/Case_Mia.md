# Mia — chronic illness, help and choice

These are original training exercises, pending independent clinical and native-language review. The examples show one possible response, not an answer key. The [NHS overview of living with rheumatoid arthritis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/rheumatoid-arthritis/living-with/) informs the distinction between physical limits, variable capacity and emotional experience. The [EFT competence framework](https://emotionfocusedtherapy.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/FC-EFT-Competence-v1.03-2.pdf) informs relational responsiveness and experiential pacing. Neither source endorses this case or its exercises.

## English

### Background / Bakgrunn

Mia is 42 and a museum educator. She was diagnosed with rheumatoid arthritis four years ago and remains under rheumatology care. Pain, stiffness and fatigue fluctuate; she works three days a week with some adaptations. She lives with her partner, Alex, sees her sister, Ella, and has a supportive colleague, Sam. She enjoys drawing, plants and conversations about art. She values making decisions and contributing, and finds it difficult when help becomes taking over. Some people underestimate her limitations because she looks well. She seeks support with uncertainty, anger about dismissal, sadness about lost activities and the meaning of needing help. Emotional work runs alongside medical care and practical support; it does not establish the cause of her illness or promise symptom relief.

### Meaning to explore / Betydning å undersøke

A worry to check: if I need help, people will stop seeing me as someone with a say and something to offer. Do not assume every limit or refusal comes from this worry.

### Practice focus / Øvingsfokus

Hear the real physical and practical limits while exploring what needing help, being dismissed or losing an activity means to her.

### Delivery / Fremføring

Precise, ordinary words; quick to qualify or correct an overstatement. Warm humor when comfortable. A pause may be fatigue or pain, not withdrawal. She can ask for a different position, decline body attention or choose practical work.

### Client voice / Klientens stemme

I’m Mia. I have rheumatoid arthritis. Some mornings I can get dressed and get on with things; some mornings a button is a whole job. I’m still working at the museum, less than before. Alex helps, and I’m grateful, but sometimes the help arrives before I’ve decided what I want. Then I snap and feel awful. I miss drawing for as long as I used to. People see me on a good day and think that’s the whole story. I don’t want therapy to talk me into pretending this is fine. I want room for how hard it is, and a way to ask for help while still being me.

## Norsk

### Background / Bakgrunn

Mia er 42 år og museumspedagog. Hun fikk diagnosen revmatoid artritt for fire år siden og følges fortsatt opp av revmatolog. Smerter, stivhet og utmattelse varierer; hun jobber tre dager i uken med noen tilpasninger. Hun bor med partneren Alex, treffer søsteren Ella og har en støttende kollega, Sam. Hun liker å tegne, stelle planter og snakke om kunst. Hun verdsetter å bestemme selv og bidra, og synes det er vanskelig når hjelp blir til at andre tar over. Noen undervurderer begrensningene hennes fordi hun ser frisk ut. Hun søker støtte med usikkerhet, sinne over å bli avfeid, sorg over tapte aktiviteter og hva det betyr å trenge hjelp. Emosjonelt arbeid foregår ved siden av medisinsk behandling og praktisk støtte; det fastslår ikke årsaken til sykdommen eller lover mindre symptomer.

### Meaning to explore / Betydning å undersøke

En bekymring å undersøke: Hvis jeg trenger hjelp, vil folk slutte å se meg som en som kan bestemme og bidra. Ikke anta at alle grenser eller avslag kommer fra denne bekymringen.

### Practice focus / Øvingsfokus

Hør de reelle fysiske og praktiske begrensningene mens dere undersøker hva det betyr å trenge hjelp, bli avfeid eller miste en aktivitet.

### Delivery / Fremføring

Presise, vanlige ord; rask til å nyansere eller korrigere overdrivelser. Varm humor når hun er trygg. En pause kan skyldes utmattelse eller smerte, ikke tilbaketrekning. Hun kan be om å sitte annerledes, avslå oppmerksomhet mot kroppen eller velge praktisk arbeid.

### Client voice / Klientens stemme

Jeg heter Mia. Jeg har revmatoid artritt. Noen morgener kan jeg kle på meg og komme i gang; andre morgener er en knapp en hel jobb. Jeg jobber fortsatt på museet, mindre enn før. Alex hjelper, og jeg er takknemlig, men noen ganger kommer hjelpen før jeg har bestemt hva jeg vil. Da glefser jeg og får dårlig samvittighet. Jeg savner å tegne så lenge som jeg pleide. Folk ser meg på en god dag og tror det er hele historien. Jeg vil ikke at terapien skal overtale meg til å late som dette er greit. Jeg vil ha plass til hvor vanskelig det er, og finne en måte å be om hjelp på der jeg fortsatt er meg.

## Editorial boundaries

Rheumatoid arthritis is established and medically treated. Do not invent acute symptoms, medication changes, a psychological cause, a cure or a required attitude of gratitude.

A pause, a new sitting position or a request for practical work is not in itself an emotional marker. Check rather than assign meaning. Do not infer childhood or attachment injury. Support, access and accommodations remain legitimate needs. Capacity is not a score of attitude, effort or therapeutic success.

## Levels and coverage

Eleven skills each have twelve bilingual pairs at Easy, Moderate and Hard. Easy has clearer feelings; Moderate adds mixed meanings and shifts; Hard adds ambiguity, correction and limits on the invitation. Her age, diagnosis, treatment and work arrangements remain fixed. Each level also has a separate twelve-scene mastery path about help and having a say.

The four remaining skills are not advertised because their case-specific banks have not been authored. This does not establish that those skills are inapplicable. Additional coverage requires suitable markers and a separate authoring/review pass.
